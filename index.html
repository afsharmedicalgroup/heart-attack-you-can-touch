Physician Evaluation Brief

Scientific Accuracy, Schematic Physiology & Engineering Audit

1. Anatomical & Pathophysiological Fidelity

  • LAD Territory: The Left Main Coronary Artery (LMCA) bifurcates into the Left Circumflex (LCx) and Left Anterior Descending (LAD) artery. The LAD courses down the anterior interventricular groove, giving off diagonal branches (anterior LV free wall) and septal perforators (anterior 2/3 of the interventricular septum).
  • Acute Plaque Rupture & Thrombus (Scene 2): Proximal LAD occlusion halts anterograde particle flow into distal LAD and diagonal branches while preserving LCx and Right Coronary Artery (RCA) perfusion.
  • Regional Wall Motion Abnormality (RWMA): Rather than global cardiac arrest, systolic contraction in the ischemic anterior/anteroseptal LV territory becomes markedly hypokinetic/akinetic, while non-ischemic basal and RV segments continue contracting with compensatory sinus tachycardia.
  • Anterior STEMI Presentation Note: Displayed anterior precordial hyperacute T-waves and convex ("tombstone") ST-segment elevation represent a classic proximal/mid-LAD occlusion pattern. Clinical presentations vary by coronary dominance, collateral circulation, wrap-around LAD anatomy, and occlusion site.

2. Reperfusion, Myocardial Stunning vs. Necrosis (Scene 3)

Percutaneous Coronary Intervention (PCI) with metallic stent deployment restores epicardial patency. However, restored macrovascular flow does not immediately normalize regional contractility:

  • Myocardial Stunning (Reversible): Viable myocytes salvaged by timely reperfusion exhibit prolonged post-ischemic contractile dysfunction due to oxygen-derived free radical generation and sarcoplasmic reticulum calcium handling stunning. Function recovers over hours to days.
  • Wavefront of Necrosis (Irreversible): Prolonged occlusion causes necrosis progressing from the subendocardium outward toward the epicardium, plus potential microvascular obstruction ("no-reflow").
